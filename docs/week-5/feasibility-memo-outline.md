# Feasibility Memo Outline

The data is suitable for the training of a baseline model as long as the highlighted limits are respected. The dataset contains the triage data associated with 55,121 emergency department encounters within the United States. It shows ESI along with 225 other features; 200 of the features relate to the presence or absence of specific chief complaints from a patient while the other 25 relate to other factors such as vital signs.

The dataset has three (3) major concerns that affect its applicability in the context of Caribbean Triage. Firstly, the dataset has no minors (people under the age of 18) in it, thus, limiting its ability to highlight paediatric trends. Secondly, the dataset is American; therefore, the cited demographics do not reflect the demographics of most Caribbean islands. For example, the majority of the encounters in the dataset involve Caucasian patients. This may cause diseases such as prostate cancer that more commonly affect African-descended populations to be ignored. Finally, the dataset has limited examples of ESI level 1 and 5, this may cause any model trained on it to focus on the more common ESI level 3 due to lack of other data.

That said, the dataset still has many qualities that make it a valuable training tool. Firstly, the dataset shows the chief complaints of patients. Many datasets only show vitals thus limiting the features that can be used in ESI prediction. Secondly, the dataset is 100% clean, that means that all 55,121 records can be used to train and test the model to optimize its accuracy. Finally, the dataset has the advantage of minimal data entry errors; most values are within the realm of plausibility.

Therefore the dataset can be used provided the following five caveats. The dataset cannot be used to train a machine learning model that can reliably triage children. Paediatric data must be sourced separately provided that legal and ethical boundaries are respected. Next, the dataset is not centred on the Caribbean; so, local patterns may be missed. Moreover, the disposition columns are included within the dataset and must be handled carefully to prevent data leakage. Furthermore, no one feature shows a strong linear relationship with ESI levels so a non-linear model must be used to mitigate that. Lastly, low number of ESI levels make a model trained on this dataset likely to be inclined to under-triage patients.
